Anniversary and gratitude
Why do we celebrate birthdays? Is it perhaps something excessive and unnecessary? Not at all! We celebrate because our birth was something good, a gift that we should remember and give thanks for each year. Otherwise, we may forget that, at the very beginning of our lives, there was an act of love.

We are temporal beings; we live in time. But we do not live only in the stretch of time between our birth and our death. When we are born, we enter into a story that was already unfolding long before us, a story that welcomes us with open arms. To understand who we are and what our place is in that story, we must become familiar with it, learn to know it, appreciate it, and give thanks for what we have received from it.

This is an act of piety. To be born in America, for example, means receiving a homeland that welcomes us and provides us with the fruits of its land, as well as a culture and a tradition that teaches us something about what it means to be human. To love America is an act of piety called patriotism, and patriotism includes gratitude. This is one of the reasons we celebrate our nation's birthday every Fourth of July: we remember and give thanks for the gift we have received.

In this same sense, while recognizing the differences, this coming Saturday we will celebrate the anniversaries of our parish and our school. We did not found them, but we have entered into their story. We have inherited their history, benefited from their gifts, and been shaped by their culture and traditions. St. Mary is part of our identity and part of our lives, and we owe it a debt of gratitude.

That is why we celebrate the 125th anniversary of the founding of our parish and the 75th anniversary of our school. Their birth was a blessing and a gift, and it is good for us to remember it. We celebrate so that we do not forget that we are not self-made: we have received much from those who came before us, and what we have received is now entrusted to us to cherish, to enrich, and eventually to pass on to those who come after us.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM

St. Francis of Assisi. Yesterday, the Church celebrated 800 years since the death of St. Francis of Assisi. While reading a little about his life, I came across this anecdote. One day, St. Francis had a vision of many churches throughout the world, and he saw people in them, praying. Moments later, he joined his confreres. He was crying. They asked him: "Why are you crying?" After he shared with them what he had seen, they replied: "Oh, why are you crying? The churches were not empty. People were praying." St. Francis explained that the prayer was the same everywhere: give me, give me, give me… "Love is not loved," he concluded. This great saint desired that God be loved. He then focused on preaching God's love to people and teaching them how to love Him. St. Mary families, let us offer the Good Lord our love, even if it is small and imperfect.

Sorry for the long note, but since you've made it this far, may I share something personal with you?

October is the month of the Rosary. When I was 7 years old, my father had a conversion. We began going to Mass every Sunday and also once or twice during the week. My dad brought us all together in my parents' bedroom. All of us, kneeling around my parents' bed, prayed the whole Rosary. Every day. All five mysteries plus the Litany of Loreto. My dad continued to pray the Rosary all his life. At the end of his life, when he no longer had the strength, my siblings and many of his grandchildren gathered around his bed to pray the Rosary. When we say to Our Lady, "Pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death," she takes our word seriously. She was very much present. If you haven't already, introduce this beautiful practice into your day.

Have a blessed week,

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM

Annual Report - Fiscal Year 2026
The financial results for fiscal year 2025–2026 are positive for both St. Mary Parish and School.

Parish offertory increased from the prior year, and total parish income exceeded both the budget and last year's results. Operating expenses were generally in line with expectations. However, maintenance remains a significant long-term concern, as our recent Reserve Study estimates the campus's annual rate of deterioration at approximately $400,000.

The school also had a positive financial year. Tuition and enrollment-related income increased from the prior year, demonstrating the continued strength and stability of our school in an increasingly challenging environment for Catholic education. Salaries and personnel expenses were well managed, and grants and scholarships exceeded expectations. As with the parish, however, facilities and maintenance require continued attention.

Overall, we have much to be grateful for. At the same time, we have an aging campus, significant future maintenance needs, and an important mission of evangelization and ministry before us. By remaining financially disciplined and strengthening our long-term stewardship and maintenance planning, we can continue to place St. Mary Parish and School on a strong financial path.

One practical way we can help address these needs is through even greater sacrificial participation in parish giving. An additional $5 per week per family, multiplied across our parish community, could make a meaningful difference in addressing our long-term needs while allowing us to continue investing in the evangelization and ministries that are central to our mission.

We should celebrate these results, thank God for His blessings, and recognize the generosity, sacrifice, and dedication of our parishioners, school families, staff, volunteers, and ministries. Everyone who prayed, gave, served, volunteered, or supported St. Mary in any way contributed to this success. May the Lord bless you abundantly for your stewardship and continued support of our parish and school.

May Our Lady watch over and bless St. Mary Parish and Virtue School.

Fr. Andrej Makovnik, DCJM
Pastor

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
