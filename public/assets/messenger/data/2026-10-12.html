Do Children Really Need a Cell Phone?
I bought my first cell phone with my own money when I was 18, and I paid my own phone bills. Today, that seems almost unthinkable. Yet giving a child a cell phone before a certain age is not a good idea. What that age should be depends on several factors: maturity, responsibility, and need. Perhaps it is precisely this last factor that ends up taking precedence over the others. Whether it is the parents who believe their children need one or the children themselves who demand one, the fact is that young people are getting cell phones at increasingly younger ages.

But what reasons could justify the need for a cell phone at an early age?

A. Communication

Communication is the original and essential purpose of the telephone.

Do our children need a cell phone to communicate? It depends on whom they need to communicate with.
  1. With their peers: Absolutely not!

    At this age, children are still learning the art of face-to-face communication, which is essential for their personal development and for acquiring social skills. A phone will not help them develop these skills; in fact, it may have the opposite effect. It gives them a platform from which to express their frustrations and share them with others without having to face anyone directly, shielded by the distance a screen provides. Not to mention that it can facilitate bullying.

    Moreover, it limits their communication to a circle of friends who, like them, are still learning how to communicate, while distancing them from their parents, who are precisely the ones who should teach them this art in the first place. And all this without even considering how little parents know about what their children share with or receive from other young people through their phones.

    Of course, children will argue that without a cell phone, they will be left out of the group and its plans. But wouldn’t it be much better for the parents themselves to coordinate with one another to organize their children’s activities?

  2. With their parents: Absolutely!

    Although, as the saying goes, you don’t need a sledgehammer to crack a nut.

    When does a child really need a cell phone to communicate with their parents? When they are at school, where they spend a good part of the day? No. When they are at home? No. When they are at a friend’s house? In principle, no: their friends have parents too, don’t they?

    For those exceptional situations in which a child really does need to contact their parents, a simple phone can always be provided. They do not need an iPhone 18 with internet access just to make a phone call.
B. Work

Definitely not. Work does not seem to be a reason that would justify giving a child a cell phone.

C. Leisure and Entertainment

Absolutely not!

This is an area that is difficult even for adults to manage. An enormous amount of time is wasted, there is a risk of developing harmful habits that later become very difficult to break, and children are deprived of the opportunity to experience their childhood fully, as they are introduced prematurely to a world that was not designed for them. To make matters worse, they are exposed to countless forms of inappropriate content.

And this is not merely a theoretical possibility. An 11-year-old child with their own cell phone and internet access can encounter pornography, intentionally or unintentionally, much sooner than their parents imagine. Filters and parental controls can help, but they are not foolproof. The most effective filter is still making sure that a child does not have their own phone with unrestricted internet access.

So, when is the right time to give children their first cell phone?

The answer depends on the parents, on how willing they are to withstand pressure from their children, and on the prevailing culture.

But if you ask me, I would say this: as long as your children do not have their own cell phone, you will retain greater control over their education and the environment in which they grow up. The day you give them one, you will have begun to lose that control.

So, if you ask me what the best age is for giving children their first cell phone, my answer is simple: the later, the better.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear School families:

We continue to lift up our voices to our Heavenly Father in thanksgiving for the gift of our parish.

St. Mary celebrated its 125th Anniversary this past Saturday. Archbishop Golka accompanied us that day, presiding at the 4:30pm Mass. During his homily he invited us to consider how many baptisms had taken place here, how many marriages… Indeed, the Lord has bestowed innumerable graces upon his people through this parish. Think about the number of conversions, healings… Take a moment to think about the favors you yourself have received: Communion, confessions, a special friendship, a particular ministry. Our school itself is a ministry of the parish!

I wanted to invite you to maintain the spirit of thanksgiving this week. Since it is the month of the rosary, you can bring these two things together. Take time this week to pray as a family a decade of the rosary (or a full rosary!) for our parish. One day you can pray for all the priests that have served at this parish (especially those who have already died), another for the families most in need, another to ask for the gift of priests and religious from our parish. Maybe another day you can pray for those who received sacraments at St. Mary and who are now far from God, that they may return to him with their whole hearts.

May our thanksgiving to the Lord increase in us our love for him and produce in us a greater appreciation for and commitment to our parish for everything the parish offers us.

God bless you,

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM
