A Night of Virtue: Industriousness
Last Saturday, we celebrated the fourth annual St. Mary Catholic Virtue School Gala, A Night of Virtue: Industriousness. To call it a tremendous success would be an understatement. The success of the evening was measured not only by the funds we raised (which exceeded our goal) but also by our ability to foster a deeper understanding of and greater support for our students with special needs.

This year’s Gala demonstrated that the St. Mary community continues to grow and is more united, stronger, and more committed than ever to our mission. The St. Mary family is a community with open hearts, ready to support those in need with magnanimity and to put its gifts and talents at the service of God and neighbor.

The messages shared that evening were powerful. They made clear that St. Mary is committed to helping students who face learning challenges and ensuring that these students and their families feel welcomed and loved for who they are, not simply for what they accomplish. They know they are not alone in their struggles, but are accompanied with love and understanding. In short, they feel at home.

The theme of the evening was industriousness and its particular significance for our students with special needs. Many of these students must work harder and persevere longer to achieve what others may accomplish with less effort. In many ways, they set the standard for industriousness at our school. Their efforts deserve our attention, respect, love, and recognition. Above all, they deserve to be taken seriously and guided toward a meaningful goal: their flourishing as persons.

These students remind us that education is about much more than acquiring knowledge or achieving academic results. It is about learning to live a good life. They, too, are called to become saints, and they desire to do so. Our mission is to accompany them, help them recognize their gifts, and guide them along the path toward the fullness of life God desires for them.

The funds raised at the Gala will help us create a dedicated space for our students with special needs, a place where they will feel welcomed, receive the support they need, and find the encouragement to persevere and grow each day.

Thank you to all the St. Mary families for your support, your magnanimity, and your trust in our mission. There is no greater work than cooperating with God in forming saints who will transform the world and lead it toward Him.

May God bless you and the entire St. Mary community!

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear School Families,

Congratulations to all those who helped put together a memorable and delightful evening last Saturday. Each of our students is a tremendous gift to our school. And as I mentioned briefly that evening—and firmly believe—your spousal love is the fountain from which the Lord brings us these blessings.

As All Saints Day approaches, we remind the students of their call to holiness. I would also like to remind you that the Lord desires to sanctify your spousal love. Always bring your love to the Lord by attending Mass together every Sunday and maybe even once during the week, going to confession together, visiting the adoration chapel, and offering a very brief prayer to Our Lord, asking Him to purify, heal, strengthen, make wise and prudent… your spousal love. How can you do this? The Lord loves a simple but intentional prayer. Hold hands, present one of these intentions, and pray one Our Father. Watch your love grow with Christ, and consider how many blessings will flow from this spousal love for the glory of God and for the good of our school and our world.

NEW THIS YEAR

I wanted to call your attention to this:

For the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed (All Souls Day) on November 2, students in 4th through 8th grade will visit the local cemetery to pray for those buried there. On the way to the cemetery, the students will pray the Holy Rosary, and once there, each student will place a flower on a grave and pray for the person buried there. The visit will last about 10 minutes, and then we will return to the school.

Did you know that visiting a cemetery between November 1 and 8 and praying for the dead can earn a plenary indulgence (applicable to the souls in purgatory)? The Church asks that we:
  1. Visit a cemetery and pray for the departed;
  2. Recite the Our Father, the Apostles’ Creed, or another standard prayer (Chaplet of Divine Mercy, Rosary, Litany of the Deceased);
  3. Fulfill the usual conditions: sacramental confession, Eucharistic Communion, and prayers for the intentions of the Pope.
Visiting a cemetery and praying for the departed is one of the seven Corporal Works of Mercy. St. Mary wants to instill this beautiful practice in every one of our students.

We have faith that our prayer will bring many from purgatory to the presence of God.

Mass offerings. St. Monica told her sons, including St. Augustine, to remember her at the altar. If you don’t currently do that, stop by the parish office and ask for Masses to be celebrated for your loved ones. There is no greater gift for those we love. Nothing has power like the Holy Mass.

Have a blessed week. God bless you,

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM
