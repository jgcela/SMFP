Why we should speak to our children about heaven
Whenever I mention death, I notice a certain discomfort in the person I am speaking with, as if they were thinking, “Why do you have to bring that up now?” At other times, they tell me directly, “Father, let’s talk about something else.” I understand: death is not an easy subject, nor does it usually make for pleasant conversation. In our culture, it has become taboo. But perhaps our reluctance to speak about death helps explain why we speak so little about holiness.

Happiness, on the other hand, is something we talk about a great deal. We all enjoy thinking about it, how to find it, and how deeply we desire it. Ideas and opinions about how to be happy are countless, and discussions about the subject can be passionate. Yet we often avoid facing the fact that we must die, perhaps because we fear that this certainty will undo our plans for happiness.

The Catholic faith offers a beautiful and hope-filled answer: complete happiness, without limits and lasting forever, exists, and it is called heaven. To reach heaven, we must become saints—that is, allow God’s grace to transform us until we reach the perfection of love and live forever in His friendship. This journey begins in this life. Those who follow this path discover that it is possible to live joyfully even amid suffering, and that suffering itself, united to Christ, can become a path of love.

These friends of God know that our life is a pilgrimage toward our eternal homeland and that, through the death and Resurrection of Jesus Christ, death can become the passage to the fullness of life with Him. That is why they dare to face it and speak about it with hope. They keep it in mind because they want to be ready to meet the Lord. What they fear most is not leaving this life, but losing communion with God forever.

If we speak to our children about heaven, the saints, and Jesus Christ our Lord, we must also speak to them about death, using words appropriate to their age and in the light of our faith in the resurrection. We can help them understand that our true and lasting home is with God, and that this life is the time we have been given to learn how to love.

We must also speak to them about hell as the eternal separation from God that comes from freely and definitively rejecting His love. We do this not to frighten them, but to help them understand that our freedom and our choices matter. We want them to learn to reject sin, trust in God’s mercy, and always seek His friendship. Holiness begins precisely there: in learning to live each day with Him, allowing Him to love us and teach us how to love.

All you holy men and women, saints of God, pray for us.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear School Families,

This morning we heard the words of St. Paul to the Romans: “Hope does not disappoint, because the love of God has been poured out into our hearts through the Holy Spirit that has been given to us.”

Last Friday we had a fantastic professional development day with our teachers, with imagination as the central theme. I will not attempt to summarize in a few lines the rich conferences that took place. What I will say is that our All Saints celebration engages our students’ imagination, because it opens a door to what is possible: holiness. And it is possible because the love of God has been poured into our hearts.

As adults, have we stopped imagining that we, too, could be saints? How often do you ask yourself what you would like to do differently? If your desire were to love God more or to be more pleasing to Him, what would you do differently? Perhaps we, too, must open our imagination to what is possible. The Lord calls you to be holy, that is, to open yourself to His love.

Rain kept us from going to the cemetery this morning, but we are working to find a way to make sure we visit the cemetery this week. We know the power of prayer, especially children’s prayer. Not all who die are ready to enter the presence of God, and for them we will offer our prayers.

“Both in life and after death, it is love that purifies… If we submit, his love softens us, straightens us out, transforms us into his own likeness… If we have not allowed his love to wash us in life… then we must consent to being drowned in these purifying waters after death.” (Sr. Ruth Burrows, Carmelite nun)

We should not wait until later to open our hearts to this love. Let us submit to it.

There is a Mass this evening at 6:30 PM. Perhaps you can make time to attend and pray for your faithful departed.

Have a blessed week. God bless you,

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM
