The magnanimous approach
St. Augustine says, in a famous sermon about bad shepherds, that they do not strengthen the weak sheep because they tell them that they have nothing to worry about and promise them prosperity simply because they are Christians. And so, when adversity comes, the sheep are not prepared to face it and become discouraged easily. Good shepherds, on the other hand, prepare them for adversity. They tell them that difficulties will come and that they will have to struggle. At the same time, however, they teach them to fix their eyes on Christ, to seek their strength in Him, and they assure them that they will have the shepherd’s own support as well.

Something similar can happen with our children. Sometimes, with the intention of reassuring them as they face the challenges of a new school year, we tell them at the beginning of the year that they have nothing to worry about, that everything will be fine, and we promise them prosperity. At the same time, we do everything in our power to make sure that this becomes a reality, moving heaven and earth so that our children will be as comfortable and well cared for as we think they should be.

When we act this way, however, we run the risk of failing to strengthen them, just as the weak sheep are not strengthened by such shepherds. It is much better to prepare our children for the difficulties they will undoubtedly have to face than to promise them that they will have no difficulties.

“You will have good moments and bad ones, easy moments and difficult ones. Fix your eyes on Christ crucified, who died for you, and offer your difficulties to Him as a sign of love. Do not be afraid or discouraged. Have a great and courageous heart for the battle of this school year. You are not alone.”

We should do this following St. Augustine’s advice: “Do not deceive them with a false hope, nor break them with panic.”

We are Catholics. We do not preach a prosperity gospel, but Christ, and Christ crucified. In Him are our hope and our joy. In Him we must place our trust in times of adversity, and with Him we must rejoice in our victories.

May the Lord grant all of us a beautiful and fruitful school year.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear St. Mary School families,
