Father Riley’s ordination
Yesterday, we celebrated the ordination of Deacon Riley Blanchard in his home parish, the very place where he was baptized and confirmed, received his First Communion, professed his religious vows, and was ordained a deacon. Deacon Riley is, quite literally, a son of St. Mary.

The world will probably say that, by choosing this path, Deacon Riley is wasting his life. In reality, he is renouncing the world and its materialistic, selfish, hedonistic, and power-seeking logic. The world cannot understand Deacon Riley’s decision because its way of thinking is completely opposed to the self-giving love that lies at the heart of priestly consecration.

Deacon Riley has become—or rather, the Holy Spirit has made him—a “man for others”: a man consecrated to helping his brothers and sisters know God, love Him, serve Him, and discover the immense love that God the Father has for us. Deacon Riley has accepted the call to be “the light of the world” and “the salt of the earth” for his brothers and sisters.

His vocation and consecration are a testimony to the fact that this life has an immense purpose, a destiny that surpasses human imagination and strength, and that goes far beyond money, success, fame, health, security, and comfort. This life is a preparation for the next; it is a pilgrimage toward heaven, eternal life, holiness. Deacon Riley is a guide, a shepherd of the flock, someone who has consecrated his life to helping us all reach the destination of this pilgrimage.

Yesterday, Deacon Riley’s father offered a toast in which he said that his son had given his fiat to God, just as the Virgin Mary had done. God had called him, and he had said, “Yes.” His father was proud, and he had every reason to be. Few things are as great as being the father of a priest.

Our parish, our school, and the DCJM have received a great gift from God in Deacon Riley’s vocation. I believe we should continue to ask God to call more young men to follow Him in this ministry. We have many young men in our school and parish. Surely, some of them will be called by God, and they will need our support and guidance.

Deacon Riley, thank you for saying “yes” to the Lord!

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear families,

This past week was jam-packed! The 8th graders and the 7th graders went on their overnight Focus retreat in Ponderosa. The aim was to focus the year and to come up with a class commitment for this year. We celebrated Mass together and had an opportunity to create new memories together. Both groups went on a hike in the morning to see the sunrise. Mornings are rough, as every parent knows. Needless to say, we missed it both times. Funny thing is that I didn't see timeliness as part of their class commitments… hmm.

For me it was an opportunity to start getting to know the students, the teachers, and the parents. We had a great group of chaperones.

On Friday, our Disciples' homes started welcoming our brother priests from Spain, Virginia and Connecticut in preparation for Fr. Riley's ordination. It was the second ordination at St. Mary (mine was the first 13 years ago, just saying!). The Lord is generous with this parish. We need to respond in kind. Today, we celebrate the Feast of St. Matthew, Apostle and Evangelist. The Lord continues to say to us: come, follow me.

Hopefully, you will ask the Lord to set His gaze upon your sons to follow Him in this special way. Continue to teach your children to recognize the voice of God, and to not be afraid to say, "Here I am, Lord."

Congratulations, Fr. Riley.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM
