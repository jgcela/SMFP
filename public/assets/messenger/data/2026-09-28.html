Virtues you need to be dependent
No one is completely independent or autonomous. Accepting our condition as “rational, dependent animals,” as Alasdair MacIntyre calls us, is difficult because it makes us terribly vulnerable. However, refusing to accept this condition is even worse, because it leads us to isolation, pride, and, very likely, failure. True education does not consist in making us independent or in teaching us to live without needing others, but rather in teaching us how to live and understand our mutual dependence.

For this task, MacIntyre proposes the “virtues of acknowledged dependence,” which enable us to recognize our own vulnerability and dependence on others and, at the same time, to respond appropriately to the vulnerability and needs of others. This recognition leads us to the first virtue: gratitude, which helps us recognize the gifts we have received from others. This virtue leads us to “just generosity,” by which we seek to respond to the gifts we have received without trying to establish an exact proportion between what we have received and what we give in return. It is the generosity of children toward the parents who have given them life and raised them. Although they can never “repay” their parents for their love, they can (and should) be generous in the way they love and respect them.

This virtue extends toward others through two virtues. First, hospitality, understood as the welcoming of both friends and strangers. We have received much from our parents, from our country, and from God, and we should be willing to welcome those who knock at our door. Second, mercy, which moves us to respond to the urgent and concrete needs of another.

In order to give and receive, other virtues are necessary. Industriousness leads us to work to obtain goods with which we can help those in need; thrift enables us to save so that we have goods to share; and discernment helps us to know how and to whom we should give, attending to those who truly need it. Finally, we need courtesy in order to receive from those who give without affection, delicacy, or generosity, and forbearance in order to accept the limitations of those who do not give enough.

Dependence is not simply a condition that we must overcome in order to achieve autonomy, but a permanent dimension of what it means to be human. Learning to depend on others makes us vulnerable, but it is precisely this vulnerability that allows others to love us as we truly are.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear school families:

We begin a week full of great saints: St. Wenceslaus (9/28); Saints Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael (9/29); St. Jerome (9/30); St. Thérèse of Lisieux (10/1); the Guardian Angels (10/2); St. Francis Borgia (10/3); and St. Francis of Assisi (10/4). In fact, we begin the month of October, dedicated to the Holy Rosary, the Missions, and Respect Life. If you do not know any of these saints, consider reading part of their works, or choose one of them and read a book about them this year. There are many sites out there, but this could be a start: https://mycatholic.life/saints/

With these feasts and devotions, we are invited to renew our call to holiness, to grow in our love and devotion to Mary, to support missionary priests, religious, and families with our prayers, to embrace our mission to bring Christ to others and to renew our spaces (home, work, society), making them more Christian, and to communicate our love of life.

Speaking of communicating our love of life, Pope Leo is completing a visit to France today. It has been a visit filled with hope. It had been about 18 years since a Pope had visited the Eldest Daughter of the Church. During his visit, he spoke to the sick and vulnerable in Lourdes in these terms: "You are not a burden. You are the beating heart of our humanity and a precious treasure for the Church." At UNESCO, he proclaimed that "Our dignity is not destroyed by illness or suffering, nor is it dependent upon our abilities. Rather, it calls for loving care at every stage of life."

Let us pray for the fruits of the month that is ending and the month that is beginning. Let us also pray for Pope Leo and for the fruits of his apostolic visit to France.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM
