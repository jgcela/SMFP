The grateful approach or how to start the new school year
Before I go to bed each night, I try to give thanks to the Lord for all the blessings He has poured over me that day, especially for the people in whom I have encountered Him. As the Preface of the Mass reminds us, it is indeed “right and just” to give God thanks, always and everywhere.

Giving thanks to God is a duty, but it is also something we are called to transform into a virtue, a virtue that shapes us into grateful people who recognize all that has been given to us and, through those gifts, recognize how deeply we are loved. As St. Paul reminds us, “What do you have that you did not receive?”

It is therefore right and just to end the school year with gratitude in our hearts. But how should we begin it?

Many would say, “Excited!” And they would be right. But why should we be excited?

Because of all the blessings we will receive throughout the year. Because of the good things and the challenges, the joyful moments and the difficult ones, all of which can help us grow. Because a new school year is an opportunity to learn, to serve, to form friendships, to overcome difficulties, and to become virtuous persons.

For this reason, the school year should begin with gratitude as well: giving thanks in advance for what lies ahead and for the opportunity we have been given to be here. In doing so, we awaken hope in our hearts and learn to look toward the future not with anxiety or the illusion that we can control everything, but with confidence in our provident Father.

We do not know what this school year will bring. But we know the One who will accompany us through it.

So, are you excited for the new school year?

Are you grateful?

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. James de Cendra, DCJM
Dear Parents,

This week we introduced a new tradition at our great school - the Memorare. The word means: remember. Generally, this will take place in the church at the end of the week.

The whole school gathered in the Church on Friday and our principal, Fr. James, led the moment of prayer, which consists of three steps:
  1. A gaze up to heaven. Putting ourselves in the presence of Almighty God, we consider the blessings received during the week. The desire is to finish the week together with greatful hearts;
  2. A gaze back. During this moment of the memorare, we recognize the ways in which we have closed ourselves to the love of God, and we ask the Lord to be merciful;
  3. A gaze forward, to the future. The student at this moment is invited to place his or her plans and desires in the heart of Jesus, asking for that which he needs.
The Memorare  ended by asking everyone to stand up and sing our school song to Our Lady.

Hopefully, you will take this practice and introduce it into your own life and your family’s, at the end of the day and at the end of the week.

In Mary, Christ; through Christ, to the Father!

Fr. Juan Espino, DCJM

PE Footwear: Non-Marking Soles Required
Our gym floor is new, and it is already showing significant marks. As stated in the Handbook, students must wear athletic shoes with non-marking soles for PE.

How to tell: most shoe boxes and online listings say "non-marking" outright. If yours doesn't, check the sole, light gum or clear rubber soles are almost always safe, while hard black or dark soles are the usual culprits. A quick test: press the sole against a clean hard floor and twist. If it leaves a gray or black streak, it will mark our gym.

Full gym uniform guidelines are in the Parent Handbook.
